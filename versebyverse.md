## **Verse by Verse Bible Study Tips**

* * *

### 7 Foundational Concepts
>1. An important truth to remember when studying verse by verse, or any method for that matter, is to first pray that God will reveal His word to you through His Holy Spirt, since Jesus said He (the Spirit) will teach you all things. No one knows the thoughts of God, except the Spirt of God.
>
>John 14:26, 1 Corinthians 2:11
>
>2. The verse and chapter numbers do not exist in the original Hebrew and Greek manuscripts. They are however a helpful way to organize the Bible, but often break up sentences and concepts that span across multiple verses. This is also true for our English punctuation.
>
>Ephesians 2:1-7 is one sentence in the Greek.
>
>3. The Bible teaches that every book, every concept, every line, and even every dotting of the letter i is benefical to us for how to live for and know God. Jewish Rabbi's say that when the Messiah comes He will even explain the spaces between the words.
>
>2 Timothy 3:16, Isaiah 28:10, Matthew 5:18
>
>4. The BEST commentary on the Bible is other parts of the Bible. If a concept doesn't seem to make sense in one verse, this means God is about to teach you something by using another verse to explain or expound the idea. We should always remember to measure each verse against the whole counsel of God, to keep in mind the bigger picture.
>
>Acts 20:27
>
>5. Different books and verses have varying degrees of reading levels. The Gospels and Acts are in story form to intentionally be easily accessible to kids and new believers. The book of James and 1st/2nd/3rd John are logical and practical instructions for living, which can be considered High School reading level. Until we are face to face with Jesus, we will only know His word in part. This should not cause us to lose hope, for He knows the amount of understanding we can handle and is protecting us. It will take ages for God to show us the exceeding riches of His grace and kindness towards us in Christ Jesus.
>
>1 Corinthians 13:9-12, Ephesians 2:7
>
>6. Each verse is not limited to only one meaning, interpretation or application. Let us not put constraint on God's words. The Bible will often use puns, metaphors, similes and rhetoric to make a point, so we should attempt to recognize when these figures of speech are employed by God, while also not using them to explain away a hard to swallow verse that pierced our soul with truth. God's word is living and powerful and spoken to us by the One who knows the thoughts and intents of the heart. A verse can speak to you one way, then speak to you entirely differently with a 2nd reading.
>
>Hebrews 4:12
>
>7. We are one body in Christ and can gain insights from how the Holy Spirit has spoken to other believers. Since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses to God's grace and mercy, both saints of the past that have left their stories and writings, and saints of today who joyfully live and suffer with us, let us consider and learn from one another to inspire the love that comes from understanding God's word. Don't just blindly believe what a book or another believer (or even your Dad) tells you about a verse, but rather search through the Scriptures to confirm if what you were told is true.
>
>Ephesians 1:23, 3:6, Hebrews 10:24, 12:1, Acts 17:11

### Some logical and common practices

- Read an entire chapter or group of verses in context, before trying to focus on one specific verse.
- Make an outline of your chosen reading to keep your mind within the context while looking for themes.
- Rephrase the entire verse in your own words as a longer or shorter version.
- Figure out who the verse is specifically targeted towards. Men? Women? Jews? Unbelievers? Believers? Everyone?
- Read confusing or archaically phrased verses in multiple translations.
- Find cross references across the Bible for the verse's words or concepts.
- Read multiple commentaries about the verse.
- Some verses are simple to understand or very precious and should simply be memorized. (Psalm 119:11)

### While reading, ask these types of questions about each verse.

- What does this passage say about God's character?
- What does this passage say about what God has done or plans to do?
- How can this verse apply to my life or another believer's life?
- How can the passage be used within a prayer?
- Would looking up a word in Greek/Hebrew help to understand the passage further?
- What does the verse not say or not mention that seems related?
- Does the verse command me to know or do something?
- How does this verse inspire me to live for God?
- Is there a promise from God that His saints can claim?
- Is God using this passage to reveal sin in my life?
- God's will for me is to be thankful and holy, how does this verse support His will? (1 Thessalonians 4:3, 5:18)
